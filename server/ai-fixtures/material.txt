Sample / Demonstration Data — not a real MoSPI publication.

This fixture stands in for a training note a statistical officer might upload. It is
written so that a grounded question generator has real material to work with: it contains
definitions, figures, a stated methodology, and a described limitation.

CONSUMER PRICE INDEX: CONCEPTS AND COMPILATION

The Consumer Price Index measures the change over time in the prices paid by households
for a fixed basket of goods and services. The base year for the current series is 2012.
Weights in the basket are derived from the Consumer Expenditure Survey and are revised
when consumption patterns shift materially.

Price data are collected from selected markets in 1181 villages and 1114 urban markets
every month. Village price collection is carried out in the first week, while urban
markets are visited across all four weeks of the month to capture within-month variation.

A Laspeyres formula is used, which holds the basket fixed at base-year quantities. This
means the index can overstate the cost of living when consumers substitute towards
cheaper alternatives, a limitation known as substitution bias. The index is therefore a
cost-of-goods measure rather than a true cost-of-living measure.

SAMPLING AND NON-RESPONSE

Non-response in a selected market is handled by carrying forward the last reported price
for a maximum of three months, after which the item is treated as temporarily
unavailable. Prolonged imputation weakens the index, so the share of imputed quotations
is published alongside the index as a quality indicator.

Seasonal items such as fresh fruit are handled by the class-mean imputation method during
months when they are out of season. An item is dropped from the basket entirely only when
it has been unavailable for twelve consecutive months.

INDEX NUMBERS: INTERPRETATION

A point-to-point inflation rate compares the index for a month with the index for the
same month of the previous year. This differs from a month-on-month rate, which compares
consecutive months and is more volatile because it retains seasonal movement.

The contribution of a group to overall inflation depends on both its price change and its
weight. A group with a small weight can show a large price rise and still contribute
little to headline inflation. Analysts should therefore read the weighted contribution
column rather than ranking groups by price change alone.

DATA QUALITY CHECKS

Every price quotation is checked against the previous month's quotation for the same
outlet. A change larger than 25 per cent triggers a validation query to the field
office before the quotation is accepted. Quotations that fail validation twice in a row
are excluded from that month's compilation and the exclusion is recorded in the audit
trail.

Revisions to a published index are made only when an error affects the index by more than
0.1 index points. Smaller corrections are carried into the next release rather than
restating a published figure, because frequent restatement erodes confidence in the
series.
