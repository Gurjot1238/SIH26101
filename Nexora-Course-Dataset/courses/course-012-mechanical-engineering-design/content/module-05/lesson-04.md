**Headlines on Product Failures:** **XBOX 360  
** ![A photo two Xbox 360 video game consoles, black and white versions.](https://assets.rpgsite.net/entities/main_images/000/000/006/sideblock_console/360_side.png) "Microsoft said Thursday that it will take a $1 billion charge as it extends the warranty on the Xbox 360, after an investigation showed the game console can be prone to hardware failures. 'As a result of what Microsoft views as an unacceptable number of repairs to Xbox 360 consoles, the company conducted extensive investigations into potential sources of general hardware failures,' Microsoft said in a statement."[1] **FitBit Force, FitBit (2014)** ![Two FitBit Force bracelets, plastic watches with small screens on one side.](https://cbi-blog.s3.amazonaws.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/FitBit-Force.jpg) This version of the activity-tracking device was causing skin irritation and even blisters so severe that it resulted in a class-action lawsuit in California.[2] **Galaxy Note 7, Samsung (2016)** ![A photo of a Samsung Galaxy Note 7 with stylus, a large smartphone.](https://s3.amazonaws.com/cbi-research-portal-uploads/2017/04/30172246/galaxy-note-innovation-failures-04.30.2018-300x169.jpg) Launched in August of 2016, the Note 7 boasted powerful hardware and had consumers chomping at the bit to get their hands on them. The anticipation quickly faded, though, as reports of Note 7s catching fire started hitting the news. By September 2, Samsung had stopped sales of the device. Next came a formal recall in the US on September 15th and a worldwide recall on October 10th.
